🗺️ Big Picture

What is an LLM?

🧠
Hello world AI →
→ is awesome !

An LLM (Large Language Model) is like a super-smart autocomplete. It reads billions of sentences, learns patterns in language, and then predicts what word comes next — over and over — until it can write essays, answer questions, write code, and hold conversations.

An LLM is a neural network — typically a decoder-only transformer — trained on next-token prediction via cross-entropy loss over a massive corpus. Self-attention mechanisms allow the model to capture long-range dependencies, while scale (parameters × data × compute) drives emergent capabilities.

The LLM paradigm rests on the neural scaling hypothesis (Kaplan et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2022): loss decreases predictably as a power law with compute, parameters, and data. Emergent capabilities (Wei et al., 2022) appear discontinuously at scale, challenging smooth extrapolation. Open questions: Are emergent abilities real or a measurement artifact? What is the theoretical basis of in-context learning? Can we build capable models without internet-scale data?

Clean Dataset

Imagine you're teaching a child to read. You'd give them millions of books, articles, and conversations. That's exactly what we do — except we collect trillions of words from the internet, books, and research papers. Then we clean it up: remove spam, duplicates, and offensive content.

The corpus must be carefully curated: Common Crawl (petabytes of web data), books corpora (Books1/2), Wikipedia, GitHub, arXiv, StackExchange, etc. Preprocessing includes: deduplication (MinHash/SimHash), quality filtering (perplexity-based), PII redaction, language detection, and document scoring. The Datatrove or Apache Spark pipelines are typical at scale.

2020The Pile — Gao et al. (EleutherAI) — 825GB diverse, high-quality text; first major open research corpus
2022ROOTS / BLOOM — BigScience — multilingual 1.6TB corpus; pioneered data governance & PII filtering at scale
2023RedPajama-1T / Dolma (AI2) — first fully open LLaMA-style training corpora; reproducible pipelines
2024FineWeb-Edu (HuggingFace) — LLM-based quality scoring; 15T tokens; showed educational content quality >> quantity
2024Phi-3 / Nemotron — Synthetic data pipelines; "textbook quality" data generation with LLMs
🔬 Active Research (2024–25)
  • Synthetic data scaling — can LLM-generated data replace web crawls? (Phi series, Nemotron-4)
  • Data mixing laws — Ye et al. showed domain mixture affects loss beyond scale
  • Data attribution — which training examples cause which behaviors? (TRAK, influence functions)
  • Decontamination — detecting benchmark leakage in pretraining corpora
  • Multimodal corpora — interleaved image-text (LAION-5B, OBELICS)
🚀 Open Research Directions
  • Can we train frontier models entirely on synthetic data? At what scale does quality collapse?
  • Optimal data curriculum — should data ordering matter? Evidence is mixed.
  • Continuous web ingestion — training on a live stream without catastrophic forgetting
  • Legal & ethical data sourcing — post-NYT lawsuit era, how do labs license training data?
  • Low-resource languages — most corpora are English-centric; multilingual coverage gaps

Tokenization

⏱ 1–2 months 👥 1–3 engineers 💰 $5K–$20K
▼

Computers can't read words — they only understand numbers. Tokenization chops text into small pieces (called tokens) and assigns each a number. "Hello" might become [72, 101, 108, 108, 111] or just [15496] depending on the method. GPT-4 uses about 100,000 different tokens.

Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) or SentencePiece iteratively merges frequent character pairs to form a vocabulary of ~32K–100K subword tokens. The tokenizer is trained on a representative subset of the corpus. Vocabulary size trades off between sequence length (larger vocab = shorter sequences) and embedding table memory. GPT-4 uses cl100k_base (100,277 tokens).

📜 Foundational Paper Timeline
2012WordPiece — Schuster & Nakamura (Google) — subword tokenization for Japanese/Korean; later adopted in BERT
2016BPE for NMT — Sennrich et al. — adapted compression algorithm for NLP; became the dominant method
2018SentencePiece — Kudo & Richardson (Google) — language-agnostic, trains directly on raw text; used in LLaMA
2022tiktoken (OpenAI) — efficient BPE implementation; cl100k_base for GPT-4; byte-level fallback
2023MEGABYTE — Yu et al. (Meta) — tokenizer-free, operates directly on raw bytes with hierarchical model
2024BLT (Byte Latent Transformer) — Meta — dynamic byte patching; matches BPE performance without fixed vocab
🔬 Active Research (2024–25)
  • Tokenizer-free models — BLT shows byte-level can match subword; cheaper inference for non-Latin scripts
  • Tokenization bias — BPE encodes numbers digit-by-digit; hurts arithmetic; studied by Nogueira et al.
  • Vocabulary transfer — can you extend a tokenizer post-hoc? Critical for multilingual adaptation
  • Morphologically-aware tokenization for low-resource languages
🚀 Open Research Directions
  • Is the tokenizer a bottleneck? Can end-to-end learned tokenization outperform BPE?
  • Optimal vocabulary size as a function of model scale — no principled theory yet
  • Tokenization for multimodal — unified discrete tokenizers for text, image, audio (e.g., VQVAE)
  • Can removing tokenization unlock better reasoning? (byte models see individual digits)

Now we design the AI's "brain". The most important invention here is called Attention — it lets the AI focus on the right words when making predictions, just like how you focus on key words when reading a sentence. We stack many layers of these attention blocks on top of each other.

The decoder-only transformer (GPT architecture) consists of L stacked blocks, each containing: multi-head causal self-attention (Q/K/V projections), followed by a position-wise FFN (typically 4× hidden dim with GELU/SwiGLU). Key design choices: RoPE vs ALiBi positional encodings, GQA vs MHA, RMSNorm vs LayerNorm, context length, and MoE vs dense FFN.

📜 Architecture Evolution Timeline
2017Attention Is All You Need — Vaswani et al. (Google) — transformer architecture; multi-head attention; positional encoding; still the backbone today
2018GPT-1 — Radford et al. (OpenAI) — decoder-only transformer for generative LM; 117M params; introduced the pretraining paradigm
2020GPT-3 — Brown et al. — 175B params; showed in-context learning; few-shot prompting without gradient updates
2022PaLM — Chowdhery et al. (Google) — 540B; Pathways system; SwiGLU FFN; parallel attention+FFN; MQA
2022FlashAttention — Dao et al. — IO-aware exact attention; 2–4× speedup; enabled longer contexts economically
2023LLaMA-2 — Touvron et al. (Meta) — GQA, RoPE, SwiGLU; open weights; became the research community's reference model
2023Mamba — Gu & Dao — Selective State Space Model (SSM); linear complexity; competitive with transformers on many tasks
2024Jamba (AI21), Zamba — hybrid transformer+Mamba architectures; best of both worlds
🔬 Active Research (2024–25)
  • Sub-quadratic attention — linear attention (RetNet, RWKV), SSMs (Mamba-2), hybrid models
  • Long context — LLaMA-3.1 1M tokens via RoPE scaling; YaRN, LongLoRA, LongRoPE
  • Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) — Mixtral 8×7B, DeepSeek-MoE; 8 experts, route 2 per token; 4× efficiency
  • Multimodal architectures — LLaVA, Gemini, Flamingo; visual/audio token integration
  • Architecture search — scaling laws for layer depth vs width (Hu et al. 2024)
🚀 Open Research Directions
  • Can SSMs (Mamba) match transformers at scale? Evidence so far mixed at 70B+
  • Truly unbounded context — current long-context is still approximate or expensive
  • Weight sharing and modularity — can we reuse sub-networks across tasks?
  • Mechanistic understanding — what do attention heads actually compute? (Elhage et al., circuits work)
  • Post-transformer architectures — is attention the right inductive bias for reasoning?

Pre-Training

This is the most expensive step. We feed the model all our data and make it predict the next word billions of times. Every time it's wrong, we adjust its internal numbers slightly. After doing this trillions of times across thousands of powerful GPUs running for months, the model learns to predict language incredibly well.

Pre-training is autoregressive next-token prediction minimizing cross-entropy loss via AdamW optimizer with cosine LR scheduling and warmup. Requires distributed training: tensor parallelism, pipeline parallelism, and data parallelism (3D parallelism). Megatron-LM, DeepSpeed ZeRO, or FSDP are standard frameworks. Chinchilla scaling laws guide optimal token/parameter ratios (≈20 tokens per parameter).

📜 Scaling & Training Timeline
2020Scaling Laws — Kaplan et al. (OpenAI) — power law relationships: L ∝ N⁻⁰·⁰⁷⁶, L ∝ D⁻⁰·⁰⁹⁵; guided GPT-3 design
2021Megatron-LM v2 — Narayanan et al. — 3D parallelism (tensor+pipeline+data); trained 1T params; enabled modern scale
2022Chinchilla — Hoffmann et al. (DeepMind) — revised scaling laws; optimal: equal scaling of params & tokens; invalidated GPT-3 training ratio
2022DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 — Rajbhandari et al. (Microsoft) — shards optimizer states/gradients/params; 10× memory reduction
2023LLaMA — Touvron et al. — trained 65B on 1.4T tokens (over-trained vs Chinchilla); showed inference-optimal ≠ training-optimal
2024MuP (Maximal Update Parameterization) — Yang et al. — hyperparameter transfer across scales; tune at small scale, apply at large scale
🔬 Active Research (2024–25)
  • Over-training vs Chinchilla-optimal — LLaMA showed inference-optimal models need more tokens than Chinchilla suggests
  • Curriculum learning — does data ordering matter? Blevins et al. showed domain ordering helps math/code
  • Continual pretraining — adding new knowledge post-training without catastrophic forgetting (DARE, TIES merging)
  • Training instabilities — loss spikes at scale; μP helps but doesn't eliminate; relationship to learning rate warmup
  • Compute-optimal frontier — DeepSeek-V2 showed MoE changes the Pareto frontier significantly
🚀 Open Research Directions
  • Do scaling laws hold past 10²⁸ FLOPs? Extrapolation is uncertain; wall may exist
  • Alternative training objectives beyond next-token prediction — masked diffusion, MDLM
  • Online/streaming training on live data without full retraining
  • Hardware-aware architecture co-design — training cost depends heavily on memory bandwidth
  • Understanding loss spikes — no consensus on cause; critical for reliable long runs

After pre-training, the model can predict text but doesn't know how to be helpful. Fine-tuning is like teaching it manners. We show it thousands of examples of good conversations — "Human asks X, Assistant answers Y" — and it learns to be a helpful assistant instead of just a text predictor.

Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) trains the model on curated instruction-response pairs (FLAN, Alpaca, ShareGPT, OpenAssistant-style data). The base model is fine-tuned on these with a lower LR and fewer steps. Parameter-efficient alternatives: LoRA/QLoRA adapt only a small adapter matrix (r=8–64), reducing trainable params by 99%+ while retaining most of the base model's knowledge.

📜 Instruction Tuning & PEFT Timeline
2021FLAN — Wei et al. (Google) — instruction tuning on 60+ NLP tasks; showed fine-tuning on task descriptions improves zero-shot generalization
2021LoRA — Hu et al. (Microsoft) — low-rank adaptation; freeze base model, train two small matrices (A,B); r×d params instead of d²
2022InstructGPT — Ouyang et al. (OpenAI) — SFT on 13K human-written examples → RLHF; showed alignment via imitation + feedback
2023Alpaca / Self-Instruct — Wang et al. — LLM-generated instruction data (52K examples from text-davinci-003); opened low-cost SFT era
2023QLoRA — Dettmers et al. — 4-bit quantized base + BF16 LoRA adapters; fine-tune 65B on a single 48GB GPU
2024LIMA — Zhou et al. (Meta) — 1000 carefully curated examples ≈ 52K random examples; "less is more" for alignment
🔬 Active Research (2024–25)
  • Synthetic SFT data at scale — Magpie, Nemotron-4 generate millions of instruction pairs autonomously
  • Data quality vs quantity — LIMA showed 1K curated examples can rival much larger datasets
  • Multimodal SFT — LLaVA-style visual instruction tuning; video, audio extensions
  • Code SFT — CodeLLaMA, DeepSeekCoder; code data improves general reasoning
  • Continual SFT — adding new skills without forgetting previous ones (catastrophic forgetting)
🚀 Open Research Directions
  • What makes SFT data "high quality"? No principled definition; IFD score, perplexity filtering are proxies
  • Self-play fine-tuning — can models improve by generating and self-evaluating their own examples?
  • Merge of SFT adapters — model merging (TIES, DARE) to combine domain experts without full retraining
  • When does SFT hurt? Evidence of "alignment tax" on some capabilities; tradeoffs not fully understood

We ask humans to rate two AI responses: "Which is better, A or B?" We collect thousands of these ratings, train a Judge AI on them, then use that judge to continuously improve our main model. This is how ChatGPT learned to sound so natural and helpful — it's basically teaching the AI using human preferences.

RLHF (InstructGPT/ChatGPT approach): (1) Train a Reward Model on human preference pairs (Bradley-Terry model), (2) Fine-tune the policy via PPO with KL-divergence penalty to prevent reward hacking. Modern alternatives: DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) eliminates the separate RM and optimizes preferences directly. GRPO (DeepSeek), RLAIF (Constitutional AI), and SimPO are further refinements.

📜 Alignment & RLHF Timeline
2017Learning from Human Preferences — Christiano et al. (OpenAI) — original RLHF on Atari; foundation of the modern approach
2022InstructGPT — Ouyang et al. (OpenAI) — RLHF on language models at scale; humans preferred InstructGPT 175B over GPT-3 175B
2022Constitutional AI — Bai et al. (Anthropic) — RLAIF; LLM critiques its own outputs using a "constitution"; reduces human annotation
2023DPO — Rafailov et al. (Stanford) — implicit reward; optimizes Bradley-Terry loss directly on policy; no separate RM or PPO
2024GRPO — Shao et al. (DeepSeek) — Group Relative Policy Optimization; removes value model; used in DeepSeek-R1 reasoning model
2024Process Reward Models — Lightman et al. (OpenAI) — reward at each reasoning step, not just final answer; enables o1-style thinking
2024SimPO — Meng et al. — reference-free DPO variant; length-normalized; simpler and competitive
🔬 Active Research (2024–25)
  • Process Reward Models (PRMs) — step-level credit assignment for reasoning; ORM vs PRM debate
  • RLVR (RL from Verifiable Rewards) — DeepSeek-R1; train on math/code with ground-truth reward; no human labels
  • Scalable oversight — can weak supervisors guide stronger models? (Burns et al., Superalignment team)
  • Reward hacking — models overfit reward model; goodhart's law at scale; Coste et al. 2023
  • Constitutional AI scaling — RLAIF can reduce human label cost by 10×; Claude's approach
🚀 Open Research Directions
  • Weak-to-strong generalization — can GPT-4 be supervised by GPT-2-level labels? (OpenAI Superalignment)
  • Debate as alignment — AI models debate; humans judge; can this scale where human evaluation fails?
  • Value alignment vs capability — are safety and capability actually in conflict?
  • Multi-objective alignment — helpfulness, harmlessness, honesty are often in tension; formal treatment lacking
  • Self-improvement loops — can models improve alignment through self-play without human labels?

Before releasing your AI, you need to test it rigorously. We run thousands of standardized tests — math problems, trivia questions, coding tasks — and compare our model's scores against other models. We also test for harmful outputs, biases, and factual errors. Think of it like a final exam before graduation.

Standard benchmarks: MMLU (57-subject knowledge), HellaSwag (commonsense), HumanEval/MBPP (code), GSM8K/MATH (reasoning), TruthfulQA, BIG-Bench Hard, MT-Bench (chat), LMSYS Chatbot Arena (ELO-based human eval). Red-teaming for safety: adversarial prompts, jailbreaks, bias audits, and Constitutional AI evaluations. Eleuther's lm-evaluation-harness is standard.

📜 Evaluation Methodology Timeline
2021BIG-Bench — Srivastava et al. (204 authors) — 204 tasks beyond current capabilities; first collaborative benchmark effort at scale
2021MMLU — Hendrycks et al. — 57 subjects from elementary to professional level; became the standard knowledge benchmark
2022HELM — Liang et al. (Stanford) — holistic evaluation: accuracy + calibration + robustness + fairness + efficiency
2023MT-Bench / Chatbot Arena — Zheng et al. (LMSYS) — GPT-4 as judge; ELO ratings via human A/B comparisons; became community standard
2024GPQA — Rein et al. — PhD-level science questions even experts get 65%; tests genuine knowledge vs pattern matching
2024SWE-Bench — Jimenez et al. — real GitHub issues; tests agent-level code editing capability; harder than HumanEval
🔬 Active Research (2024–25)
  • Benchmark contamination — MMLU, GSM8K likely in pretraining data; inflated scores; decontamination methods active area
  • LLM-as-judge — GPT-4 judge has positional bias, verbosity bias, self-enhancement bias (Zheng et al.)
  • Agent evaluation — SWE-Bench, WebArena, OSWorld test real task completion, not just QA
  • Capability elicitation — can we reliably surface all of a model's capabilities? Chain-of-thought, few-shot changes scores significantly
  • Safety red-teaming — automated red-teaming (Perez et al.), StrongREJECT for jailbreak evaluation
🚀 Open Research Directions
  • Evaluation-proof benchmarks — can we create benchmarks that can't be contaminated? Dynamic, generated-at-eval-time problems
  • Real-world task evaluation — browsing, coding, research assistance; how to standardize?
  • Behavioral consistency — does the model behave the same when deployed vs evaluated?
  • Interpretability as evaluation — mechanistic understanding of capabilities vs black-box measurement

Now we make it available to users. This means putting the model on powerful servers, compressing it so it runs faster, building an API so apps can talk to it, and scaling up so thousands of people can use it simultaneously. It's like opening a restaurant — you need the kitchen (servers), menu (API), and enough staff (infrastructure) to serve everyone.

Inference optimization: quantization (AWQ, GPTQ, bitsandbytes INT4), speculative decoding, FlashAttention-2, PagedAttention (vLLM), continuous batching, tensor parallelism across GPUs. Serving frameworks: vLLM, TGI, TensorRT-LLM, Triton. For production: rate limiting, safety filters, streaming tokens, observability (latency, TTFT, TPS metrics), and cost per token optimization.

📜 Inference Systems Timeline
2022FlashAttention — Dao et al. (Stanford) — fused CUDA kernel; IO-aware; 2–4× speedup; enabled 10K+ context training
2023Speculative Decoding — Chen et al. (Google) / Leviathan et al. — small draft model proposes tokens; large model verifies in parallel; 2–3× speedup
2023vLLM / PagedAttention — Kwon et al. (UC Berkeley) — virtual memory for KV cache; near-zero KV fragmentation; 24× throughput
2023AWQ — Lin et al. (MIT) — activation-aware weight quantization; INT4 with minimal accuracy loss; hardware-friendly
2024FlashAttention-3 — Shah et al. — asynchronous overlapping of compute and memory on H100; 1.5–2.0× over FA-2
2024SGLang — Zheng et al. — structured generation language; radix attention for KV reuse across requests; 5× throughput
🔬 Active Research (2024–25)
  • Speculative decoding variants — Medusa (multi-head), Eagle (autoregressive draft), SpecInfer; 3–5× practical speedups
  • KV cache compression — StreamingLLM (attention sinks), SnapKV, PyramidKV; compress KV without accuracy loss
  • Edge deployment — MLC-LLM, llama.cpp run 7B on phones; Apple Neural Engine, Qualcomm AI; quantization to 2-4 bits
  • Disaggregated serving — separate prefill and decode across machines (Splitwise, Mooncake)
  • Multi-model serving — heterogeneous clusters; routing queries to right-sized models
🚀 Open Research Directions
  • Hardware-software co-design — Groq, Tenstorrent, Cerebras custom chips; will they displace H100s?
  • Continuous batching at scale — dynamic sequence lengths; optimal scheduling is an open problem
  • Neuromorphic approaches — Intel Loihi, IBM NorthPole; spike-based inference could be 100× more efficient
  • Memory bandwidth wall — LLM inference is memory-bound; DRAM bandwidth limits scale; HBM4, CXL solutions emerging

🔬 Research Frontier

The Research Landscape

What's solved, what's open, where to focus your PhD or research career

🧩 Grand Open Problems in LLM Research

🎯
True Reasoning
Can LLMs genuinely reason, or is it sophisticated pattern matching? Failure on novel logical forms (Winogrande variants, ARC-Challenge) suggests current limits. Chain-of-thought helps but doesn't solve compositionality. Key papers: Dziri et al. 2023 "Faith and Fate", Bubeck et al. 2023 "Sparks of AGI".
Status: Deeply open
🔄
Continual Learning
LLMs forget old knowledge when trained on new data (catastrophic forgetting). Current solutions: replay buffers, EWC, LoRA mixing — all partial. No clean solution at scale. Active area: DARE, TIES merging, gradient projection methods.
Status: Active area
🕵️
Interpretability
We cannot explain why models produce specific outputs. Mechanistic interpretability (Anthropic's circuits work, superposition hypothesis) is making progress at toy scale. Scaling to production models is unsolved. Critical for safety and trust.
Status: Early stage
📏
Reliable Calibration
Models express false confidence. RLHF makes calibration worse (Kadavath et al. 2022). Temperature scaling helps at small scale. At deployment scale, models confidently hallucinate. Key work: "Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know".
Status: Partially solved
⚖️
Scalable Alignment
Current RLHF requires human labels that may not scale to superhuman capabilities. Debate, amplification, scalable oversight are theoretical proposals. Superalignment team at OpenAI, MIRI, ARC working on this. No solution validated at scale.
Status: Unsolved
🌐
Long-Horizon Agency
Agents that can plan and execute tasks over many steps (SWE-Bench requires 50+ steps). Current models fail on tasks requiring persistent memory, error recovery, and goal-directed planning. ReAct, Reflexion, AutoGPT are starting points, not solutions.
Status: Emerging

📚 Essential Paper Reading List for LLM Researchers

2017
Attention Is All You Need — Vaswani et al.
The transformer paper. Must read. Every LLM traces back to this.
2018
GPT-1: Improving Language Understanding by Generative Pre-Training — Radford et al.
Established the pretraining paradigm. Surprisingly short and readable.
2018
BERT — Devlin et al.
Encoder-only counterpart; MLM objective; contextualized embeddings. Understand to appreciate GPT's differences.
2020
Language Models are Few-Shot Learners (GPT-3) — Brown et al.
In-context learning discovery. The paper that changed the field's trajectory.
2022
Chain-of-Thought Prompting — Wei et al. (Google)
Step-by-step reasoning emerges at scale. Showed prompting strategy matters enormously.
2020
Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models — Kaplan et al.
Power law relationships across compute, data, parameters. Foundation for budgeting any training run.
2022
Training Compute-Optimal LLMs (Chinchilla) — Hoffmann et al.
Revised Kaplan's laws; showed GPT-3 was undertrained. Changed how every lab thinks about training budgets.
2022
Emergent Abilities of LLMs — Wei et al.
Capabilities appear suddenly at scale thresholds. Controversial: Schaeffer et al. 2023 argue it's a measurement artifact.
2023
LLaMA: Open Foundation LLMs — Touvron et al.
Over-trained small models beat large ones at inference. Changed community's focus to inference-optimal training.
2024
DeepSeek-V2 — DeepSeek AI
MoE + MLA attention; showed non-dense architectures shift the scaling Pareto frontier significantly.
2022
Training Language Models to Follow Instructions (InstructGPT) — Ouyang et al.
The RLHF paper that made ChatGPT possible. SFT + RM + PPO pipeline spelled out clearly.
2022
Constitutional AI — Bai et al. (Anthropic)
RLAIF: use AI feedback instead of human labels. First practical alternative to expensive human annotation.
2023
Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) — Rafailov et al.
Showed RLHF's PPO loop is unnecessary; optimize preference directly on the LM loss. Now widely adopted.
2023
Weak-to-Strong Generalization — Burns et al. (OpenAI)
Can a GPT-2 supervisor elicit GPT-4 capabilities? Preliminary "yes" — critical for scalable oversight.
2024
DeepSeek-R1 — DeepSeek AI
RLVR on verifiable rewards (math, code) without SFT. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). Reproduces o1-style reasoning.
2021
LoRA — Hu et al. (Microsoft)
Low-rank adaptation. Trains 0.1% of parameters; near-full fine-tune quality. Used everywhere.
2022
FlashAttention — Dao et al. (Stanford)
Tiling + kernel fusion for exact attention. 2–4× faster, 10× less memory. Necessary reading for systems work.
2023
QLoRA — Dettmers et al.
4-bit quantized base + 16-bit adapter. Fine-tune 65B on a single 48GB GPU. Democratized alignment research.
2023
PagedAttention / vLLM — Kwon et al. (UC Berkeley)
Virtual memory for KV cache; 24× throughput improvement. Now the default serving framework.
2024
GaLore — Zhao et al.
Gradient Low-Rank Projection; reduces optimizer memory 65%; enables pre-training on consumer hardware.
2024
Mamba-2 — Dao & Gu (Princeton/CMU)
SSM with structured state space duality; bridges SSMs and attention theoretically; competes with transformers on long-context.
2024
Byte Latent Transformer (BLT) — Meta
Tokenizer-free LLM; dynamic byte patching; matches BPE performance; better on rare words and code.
2024
Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute — Snell et al. (Berkeley)
More inference compute can substitute for more training compute; process reward models key; basis for o1/o3 approach.
2025
DeepSeek-R1 — DeepSeek AI
Pure RL from verifiable rewards; emergent chain-of-thought without SFT bootstrapping; publicly available weights.
2025
Gemini 2.0 / Claude 3.7 / GPT-4o (technical reports)
Current frontier; study architecture choices, training insights, and safety approaches disclosed in each report.

🧭 Research Entry Points — Where to Start

🎓
PhD / Postdoc Entry
1. Master the transformer from scratch (Karpathy nanoGPT)
2. Reproduce 1 landmark result (DPO, LoRA, speculative decoding)
3. Find one underexplored sub-problem in the area you reproduced
4. Submit to ICLR / NeurIPS / ICML / ACL with a clear contribution
5. Open-source your code and engage with the community (Twitter/X, Discord)
🔭
Highest-Leverage Research Areas (2025)
📌 Test-time compute scaling — still very early; process reward models, search methods
📌 Interpretability — Anthropic's circuits work; mechanistic understanding of transformers
📌 Data quality & synthetic data — what makes data good? No principled theory
📌 Long-context reasoning — 1M tokens possible; comprehension at scale unsolved
📌 Multi-agent systems — LLMs as collaborating agents; evaluation, training, coordination
📌 LLM theory — why does in-context learning work? What are transformers computing?
🛠️
Systems / ML Engineering Research
📌 Inference optimization — speculative decoding variants, KV cache compression
📌 Distributed training — new parallelism strategies for post-H100 hardware
📌 Quantization theory — why does INT4 work so well? Theoretical grounding lacking
📌 Memory-efficient training — GaLore successors; gradient compression
📌 Hardware co-design — custom silicon for attention-free or MoE architectures
